### 1. Executive Framing and Gravity-Tax First Principles
Gravity tax is the irreducible energetic cost of moving mass against gravitational potential and delta-v budgets. From the rocket equation and orbital mechanics, bulk transfer of low-value mass from LEO to the lunar surface is inefficient. Primary processing must therefore occur in LEO on continuous-solar-powered ISAM platforms. Only high-value-density refined products, finished modules, propellant, or electronics that cannot be economically matched by lunar ISRU justify the cislunar bridge.

SpaceXSI MoonBaseAlpha’s six-legged symbiotic architecture (Hungry Hippo Leg 0 + five surface legs) is explicitly designed around this constraint: capture and refine in LEO, transfer selectively, close material and energy loops on the lunar surface via FAIRY DUST fabrication. This synthesis prioritizes materials and architectures that maximize net value per unit of gravity tax paid.

### 2. Highest-Value LEO Debris Feedstocks for Hungry Hippo
LEO debris mass is dominated by aluminum alloys (~35–40 %), with meaningful titanium (~4–5 %), copper/wiring/electronics, stainless steels, residual propellants, and specialty materials. Forecasts indicate aluminum and titanium inventories in key LEO altitude bands will continue to grow through 2050 even under conservative post-mission disposal assumptions.

**Priority ranking for harvest and processing (value density × processability × lunar complementarity):**

1. **Aluminum alloys (primary volume driver)**  
   Structures, tanks, panels. Easily melted and recast in vacuum with solar or inductive heating. Outputs: structural alloys, radiators, solar-array frames, 3D-print feedstock, radiation-shielding layers, and FAIRY DUST permanent linings for MOONWORM tunnels. Pre-alloyed scrap (6061, 7075-class) offers higher immediate utility than primary lunar aluminum extracted from anorthite.

2. **Titanium alloys**  
   High strength-to-weight, fatigue resistance, and corrosion performance. Ideal for critical load-bearing elements, landing hardware, high-stress joints, and radiation-hardened components. Lunar titanium extraction (ilmenite routes) is more energy-intensive in early phases; refined LEO Ti clears the transfer threshold.

3. **Copper, harnesses, and recovered electronics**  
   Conductivity and electronics content. Lunar regolith copper is scarce (tens of ppm). Recovered copper supplies bus bars, thermal straps, and power distribution; electronics yield sensors, radiation-tolerant parts, and silicon feedstock that accelerate StarMind/AI1 surface integration.

4. **Residual volatiles and propellants**  
   Highest immediate utility. Residual hydrazine, NTO, cryogenics, water, or battery electrolytes become propellant for orbital tugs, surface hoppers, or methalox feedstock. Zero additional extraction energy once captured.

5. **Stainless steels and select specialty alloys**  
   Useful for tools, fasteners, ballast, or alloy blending. Lower priority than Al/Ti/Cu because lunar iron is more accessible, but pre-formed high-alloy components still save processing steps.

Bulk unprocessed scrap, low-grade slag, CFRP (energy-intensive separation), insulation, and paint remain in LEO for local orbital factories or are discarded after high-value extraction. Capture prioritizes large catalogued objects and dense debris clouds (nets, tethers, harpoons, ADRV/ISAM) over diffuse small debris.

### 3. Selective Cislunar Transfer Rule
Transfer only what justifies the energy cost:
- Refined aluminum and titanium alloys for structural and thermal performance that accelerate early habitat and railgun infrastructure.
- Copper and electronics for power and compute layers that lunar ISRU cannot rapidly supply at scale.
- Propellant for immediate logistics or surface operations.
- Finished modules or high-value components whose Earth-launch equivalent cost exceeds transfer cost.

Everything else stays in LEO. Energy balance: continuous solar for orbital processing; lunar excess power/propellant supports only selective transfers. Grok optimizes targeting, processing sequences, and energy-aware routing. Outputs feed FAIRY DUST and direct StarMind assembly lines.

### 4. International Space Station as Special Case
The ISS (~420–450 t) is the single highest-value concentrated LEO target. Composition is aluminum-dominated with significant titanium, copper, radiators, solar arrays, batteries, and electronics—exactly the priority list above.

**Official baseline (as of late 2026):** Controlled destructive deorbit into a remote Pacific corridor (Point Nemo region) around 2030–2031 via SpaceX U.S. Deorbit Vehicle after gradual altitude lowering. Primary structure is approaching original design life; modules were not designed for easy disassembly; international ownership and liability considerations favor safe deorbit. Major module reuse has been evaluated and largely rejected for cost, logistics, and lack of commercial interest. Limited smaller artifacts may be returned for preservation.

**Engineering opportunity under a Hungry Hippo / circular-space policy shift:**  
If partners open a window before final lowering begins (~2028 onward), the ISS becomes the ultimate large-derelict harvest. Selective robotic disassembly or in-orbit foundry processing of aluminum, titanium, copper, and electronics would supply high-value feedstock while clearing a major debris risk. Residual radiators and power systems could support temporary ISAM platforms. Gravity-tax advantage is extreme because the mass is already in LEO. Commercial concepts treating the station as a non-habitable salvage yard/ISAM workspace exist but are not current policy.
