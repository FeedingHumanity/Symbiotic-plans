## Subsurface Physics Application: Dark Matter Direct Detection (LUX-ZEPLIN Scale)

### 1. The Terrestrial Limitation vs. The Lunar Solution
Terrestrial Weakly Interacting Massive Particle (WIMP) detectors, such as the LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) experiment, are physically limited by the "neutrino floor." High-energy atmospheric neutrinos, created by cosmic rays striking Earth's thick atmosphere, easily penetrate deep underground mines (e.g., SURF in South Dakota). These neutrinos interact weakly within liquid xenon cryostats, producing nuclear recoils that are visually indistinguishable from predicted dark matter signals.

Because the Moon lacks a sprawling atmosphere, cosmic rays impact solid lunar regolith directly. This alters the resulting particle decay chains and drastically reduces the ambient flux of interfering high-energy neutrinos. Deploying an ultra-pure liquid xenon detector inside a Prufrock Moonworm tunnel allows particle physics to bypass Earth’s neutrino floor completely, unlocking the next order of magnitude in detector sensitivity.

### 2. Prufrock Moonworm Structural Suitability
The invariant physics and targeted lunar adaptations of the Prufrock Moonworm architecture solve the strict environmental constraints required for cryogenic, zero-background physics facilities:

*   **Engineered Mass Shielding**: Deep-boring capabilities utilize the compressed density of the lunar regolith to naturally absorb cosmic radiation, matching or exceeding the cosmic-ray attenuation of deep terrestrial mines.
*   **ISRU Sintered Linings for Cleanroom Integrity**: Liquid xenon detectors tolerate zero external contamination (allowing <1 gram of dust within the core assembly to avoid trace radioactivity). The Moonworm’s real-time In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) sintering and extrusion system melts jagged, electrostatically charged regolith into an impermeable, glassy structural lining. This seals the laboratory volume and isolates hyper-sensitive equipment from invasive lunar dust.
*   **Targeted Cold-Trap Deployment**: Utilizing modular, Starship-compatible TBM deployment allows deep laboratory excavation to be strategically sited near the lunar poles. This geography provides co-located access to permanent solar/nuclear surface power grids necessary to continuously maintain the heavy xenon cryostat at its stable operating temperature of minus 108°C.
