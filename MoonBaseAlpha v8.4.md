- Delta-v cost of bulk mass transfer LEO to lunar surface is high. Move only high-value refined products or precision modules.  
- Earth-orbit solar power is continuous; thermal rejection is the limiting factor. Process debris primarily in Earth orbit (Leg 0) to feed both StarMind component production and selective lunar transfer. LEO manufacturing is powered primarily by continuous in-orbit solar arrays on Hippo/ISAM platforms (vacuum radiative cooling available). Selective lunar power support is limited to high-value transfer propellant or emergency loads only.

Use lunar mass and energy for the base itself, for AI1 support fabrication, and for powering the magnetic railgun.  
- Waste heat is not rejected — it is captured, stored, and redistributed via the Failproof Segmented Tri-Leg Thermal Architecture.  
- StarMind nodes themselves are high-value products: large solar arrays, onboard processors (initially NVIDIA Rubin GPUs + Vera CPUs under the exclusive SpaceX–NVIDIA partnership), radiators, and laser links. Their production must close mass/energy loops or the constellation cannot scale economically.  
- Lunar escape velocity is only approximately 2.4 km/s with no atmosphere. An electromagnetic mass driver (magnetic railgun) converts electrical energy (from Leg 4 solar/nuclear + thermal recovery + repurposed Raptors) directly into kinetic energy for AI1 payloads, eliminating the need for chemical propellant on every launch and enabling far higher cadence than pure Starship export.

0. Resilience Backbone: Failproof Segmented Tri-Leg Thermal Topology (Tri-Web)

To achieve absolute zero-point-failure thermal and fluid distribution, MoonBaseAlpha implements the Segmented Tri-Leg (Tri-Web) Topology. This is the circulatory system of the entire closed-loop architecture.

Architectural Blueprint

1. Three Active Geographic Arteries (No Idle Lines)  
Arteries 1 (West), 2 (Center), and 3 (East) routed along completely separate geographic paths, paralleling MOONWORM tunnels.  
- Normal operations: each artery carries exactly 33.3 percent of total thermal/fluid load.  
- Failproof sizing rule: each artery is dimensioned to carry 50 percent of maximum global peak flow. Loss of any one artery automatically up-scales the remaining two to 100 percent capacity with zero downtime. Idle-line freezing risk is eliminated by design.

2. Cross-Tie Interconnect Matrix  
Underground cross-tie lines every 200 m equipped with fast-acting pneumatic isolation valves (FAIVs).  
- Localized damage triggers instantaneous isolation (target 45 ms or less via piezoelectric sensors).  
- Fluid and thermal load shunt through cross-ties, bypassing the damaged segment while the remainder of the artery and the other two arteries stay fully operational.

3. Localized Phase-Change Material (PCM) Thermal Batteries  
Every biological module, habitat pod, critical industrial node, and railgun power electronics bay is wrapped in Lithium-Nitrate Trihydrate (LiNO3·3H2O) PCM matrix.  
- Phase-change temperature 303 K (30 °C).  
- Maintains survivable temperatures (290 K or higher) for 72 hours or more under total disconnection.

Failure Response Matrix  
- Localized artery puncture → FAIV isolation + cross-tie reroute → 100 percent operational.  
- Major cave-in on one artery → geographic isolation + remaining two arteries scale to 50 percent each → 100 percent operational.  
- Total power blackout → node-level emergency pumps + PCM passive mode → core life support retained.  
- All arteries severed → pod-level isolation + 72-hour PCM survival state → Leg 5 assets execute autonomous repair.

Arteries are co-routed with MOONWORM tunnels. Waste-heat sources feed the Tri-Leg bus. Thermal priority cascade remains: habitat survival → ISRU/process heat → electrical efficiency → radiative rejection only as last resort.
