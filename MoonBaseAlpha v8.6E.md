**The ISS feedstock sequence is a first-principles, minimum-energy path that treats the International Space Station as already-paid LEO mass.**  

The station (~420–450 tonnes) has already paid the ~9.3–9.5 km/s Earth-to-LEO delta-v cost. Recovering high-value pieces therefore only requires the remaining ~5.9–6.1 km/s to the lunar surface. This is the irreversible energetic advantage. The sequence deliberately avoids melting, furnaces, powder processing, or inventing new vehicles.

### Core Physics Constraints
- Bulk low-value mass stays in LEO (continuous free solar power, easy thermal rejection).
- Only dense, high-value refined metal (aluminum structure, titanium fittings, copper bus bars/cables, intact solar-array sections) is moved.
- Starship’s existing high-capacity, refuelable architecture is used at its natural scale.
- Progressive disassembly can stop at any point; residual mass can still be de-orbited.

### The Four-Step Sequence

**1. Propulsion independence + selective mechanical harvest**  
Keep the station at operational altitude with Dragon-trunk or dedicated reboost kits so it does not begin the final deorbit burn.  
Use Optimus-class robots (or Canadarm2 + free-flyers) equipped with cold mechanical cutters to remove the largest contiguous pieces of:
- Aluminum primary structure  
- Titanium fittings and high-stress joints  
- Copper bus bars and cabling  
- Intact solar-array sections  

No melting, no high-temperature processing, no powder. The goal is the largest usable pieces while generating the least new debris.

**2. Staging without full Starship–ISS docking**  
Full Starship is too large and its thruster plumes too energetic for direct docking to the current ISS. Practical alternatives that still obey first principles:
- Soft-capture or berth a smaller intermediate vehicle (or a Starship nose/cargo-pod section) to an existing IDSS port or a temporary truss attachment.
- Transfer the harvested pieces robotically or by short free-flight hop.
- Load them into a Starship cargo variant already waiting in a nearby LEO parking orbit.

**3. Orbital refueling + lunar transfer**  
One or more tanker Starships top off the cargo Starship in LEO — exactly the same propellant-transfer architecture already required for the Starship Human Landing System.  
The cargo Starship then performs:
- Trans-lunar injection  
- Optional lunar-orbit insertion  
- Powered descent to a pre-selected landing site near the South Pole (or a future MoonBaseAlpha site)

Because the payload is already dense metal, the propellant cost per useful kilogram is minimized.

**4. Lunar delivery and immediate use**  
Soft-land the large structural sections, bus bars, and solar-array pieces.  
They become ready-to-use feedstock for:
- Habitat frames and pressure shells  
- Radiators  
- Power distribution  
- Radiation shielding  

This skips the early, energy-intensive lunar ISRU steps that would otherwise be required to extract the same metals from regolith.

### Why This Is the First-Principles Minimum
Energy is spent only on the remaining delta-v and on mechanical separation. No new vehicle class is invented. The schedule advantage is large: early tonnes of refined metal accelerate closed-loop lunar industry far more than waiting for full ISRU maturity. Risk is bounded — progressive disassembly can be halted at any time and the residual station mass can still follow the planned controlled deorbit.

Any more complex path (full melting in LEO, multiple intermediate depots, or attempting to land the entire station) adds energy, mass, and failure modes without changing the fundamental gravity-tax arithmetic.
